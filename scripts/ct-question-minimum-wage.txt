A mid-sized city council is considering raising its local minimum wage to roughly 40 percent above the national floor over three years, and wants to know what to expect. Work out what the empirical evidence actually establishes. Cover: what the major studies and meta-analyses find on employment effects, and why credible researchers reach different answers; how the research designs differ (border-discontinuity comparisons, synthetic control, national panel studies) and what each design can and cannot detect; what happens to hours, scheduling, turnover, prices and business entry and exit as distinct from headcount; how effects vary with the ratio of the new wage to the local median wage; what the evidence says specifically about smaller employers and about younger and lower-skilled workers; and what is known about local-level increases in particular, where workers and businesses can move across a nearby boundary. Then set out what this council should realistically expect, which outcomes the evidence can predict with reasonable confidence and which it genuinely cannot, what to monitor after implementation, and what would make this city's situation differ from the places already studied. Be explicit about where economists genuinely disagree rather than presenting one side as settled.
