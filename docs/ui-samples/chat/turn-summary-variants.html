Design exploration, part 2

Three more ways to summarize a turn

The "Worked for 4.8s · 8 tool calls" line I showed you is one option, not the only one. Same real sound-effects turn, three different ideas for what that one collapsed line should actually say.

A

Progress Chips

answers "what changed" at a glance

Instead of a sentence, a row of small chips — one per file actually touched, with a count. You see what got changed without reading anything or expanding at all. Best when you mainly care about "did it touch the right files," not the blow-by-blow.

B

Timeline Dots

rhythm over reading

A tiny dot per step — green for a read/inspect, blue for an edit — packed into the same line as the headline. You feel "there were 8 steps, mostly edits" in about a third of a second, without reading a number. Weakest at explaining what happened; strongest at a quick "how much work was this" read.

C

Narrative Recap

most work, most useful

Not a stat line at all — a real one-sentence summary of what actually happened, written the way you'd describe it to someone else. This is the one I'd actually push for: it directly answers "what did it do" instead of making you infer that from a count. The tradeoff is real — it needs the model (or a cheap follow-up summarization pass) to generate this, not just tally tool calls, so it's more work to build than A or B.
