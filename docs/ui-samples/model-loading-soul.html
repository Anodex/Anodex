Model loading, with a little more soul

Today the download bar in AI & Models is a flat fill — the most "dead" moment in an otherwise atmospheric app. These directions borrow from the same visual language as the startup jump and the empty-chat backgrounds (the accent blue/violet pair, the halo breathing, the traveling spark) instead of inventing something new. 01–03 are the original independent options; 04 is 02 and 03 combined, added after comparing them side by side.

All four keep the actual progress number legible and none of them run a live particle simulation — cheap, CSS-only, safe to have several running at once if you queue multiple downloads.

Every card below is live — a full 0% → 100% → complete cycle, looping, so you can watch each direction's effects at every stage instead of a single frozen percentage.

Ember Trail

A slow sheen sweeps through the filled portion, and a small spark rides the leading edge — the same specular-sweep language as the startup mark's .sheen, and the same warm-white/blue/violet glow as its spark particles.

Best fit if: you want the safest, cheapest option — works identically at 2% or 98% progress, reads fine in a dense list of five simultaneous downloads, easiest to build.

02

Starlit Track

The unfilled track is a dim scatter of the startup field's actual star tones; the filled portion is the cyan→blue→violet ramp with brighter stars showing through, like light catching up to the field as bytes arrive.

Best fit if: you want the most literal callback to the constellation/startup DNA. The most visually rich of the three, and the one place this brings back the original three-stop gradient — deliberately, as a rare accent rather than the everyday palette.

Real tradeoff, found while checking both themes: the track is deliberately always- dark, same as the startup overlay — and that reads as rich texture next to Anodex's near-black chrome, but as a flat black bar once the surrounding UI switches to Anodex Light. It would need its own light-mode star tones to hold up there, which is more to build and tune than the other two directions need.

03

Charging Halo

The progress bar itself stays plain and fast to read — all the personality moves to the model's icon, which breathes a slow halo while active (the startup mark's exact haloBreath timing) and blooms once, briefly, the instant the download finishes — an arrival, not just a checkmark.

Best fit if: you want the numbers to stay boring/legible (never obscuring real progress with an effect) while still getting a distinct "something happened" moment on completion. Also the easiest of the three to reuse verbatim for model-load-into-memory, not just downloads.

04

Starlit Halo — 02 + 03

Exactly what it sounds like: Starlit Track's constellation-reveal bar, unchanged, plus Charging Halo's breathing icon and completion bloom layered on top. Nothing new was built for this — it's the same two rule sets applied to one card, which is why it was cheap to try.

What this actually buys you: personality both during the download (the track) and at the moment it finishes (the bloom), instead of picking one. The tradeoff is the inverse of Charging Halo alone — this is the busiest of the four, worth checking against a real queue of several downloads at once before committing, not just one card in isolation.

Also fixed here: Starlit Track's light-mode weakness from before. Rather than carry it into the combination, the always-dark starfield now degrades to the same plain track every other direction uses once the surrounding UI is in Anodex Light — the gradient fill still runs in both themes, just without forcing a texture that never had a good light equivalent. That fix applies to plain Starlit Track above too; switch your OS/browser theme and both now hold up.
